## Summary

Decision: `scan` is a form field, like upload. It is a row with [scan pages] that opens a scanner with four steps. Capture: the live camera, with the page's edges drawn in green once found, and a manual shutter. Corners: the still photo, with four handles you can drag (or move with the arrow keys) and a loupe while you drag. Review: the corrected A4 pages, which you can reorder, retake, re-corner, rotate or delete. Done: one A4 PDF row that goes up through the file handler and sends its id. On a wide screen the page strip sits in a right column. Default look, no new colour, no new dependency. One page must take 400 ms at most from shutter to corrected image.
Screens: the flow (capture, corners, review, done in the form) and a sheet of every state (no edges, no camera, camera blocked, page limit, making the PDF, upload failed, held from an earlier visit, the app's error, off, Thai), each light and dark at 390 and 1440.
Open questions: (1) For now, write our own small PDF writer (it only places JPEG pages on A4 sheets) and switch to `quire` once that exists? I recommend yes. (2) Is a form field right, rather than a block that stands alone? I recommend the field: its output is a file id the form submits, the same as upload.

## What impl builds

- **Wire**: `ScanField` in `kind/field/`, next to `UploadField`. It has the common field keys plus `max` (pages, default 20) and `file` (`FileRef`, the PDF it holds). The action carries a list holding one id, the same as upload, so `is filled` means "scanned". The PDF goes to the same file handler (`file` / `onFile` / `.onFile`) with what it is for and the type `application/pdf`. Its name is the label in kebab case plus `.pdf`. The words (the en/th tables in `scan.js`) go in the field's words table.
- **The web chunk**: `web/src/render/field/scan.ts` draws the row. [scan pages] loads a lazy chunk the first time it is tapped (at most 25 KB gzipped). That chunk holds the scanner view and a worker. The worker does all the image work: decoding, finding the corners on a copy at most 640 px long (grey, blur, edges, the largest four-sided outline), the perspective warp to A4 at 150 dpi (1240 × 1754), JPEG at quality 0.8 through `OffscreenCanvas.convertToBlob`, and putting the PDF together. The main thread only draws.
- **Perf budget, one page**: shutter to corrected image at most **400 ms** at p95 on the CI perf runner, for a 4032 × 3024 fixture photo. That splits into finding the corners on 640 px in at most 40 ms and the warp plus JPEG in at most 300 ms. The live outline costs at most 30 ms a frame and runs at most 10 times a second, dropping frames while busy. No main-thread task runs longer than 16 ms. Putting the PDF together takes at most 50 ms a page.
- **Dependencies, for the owner**: none new. On the web: the corner finder, the warp and a small PDF writer (JPEG pages on A4) are our own TypeScript, and JPEG encoding is the browser's. Once `quire` exists it can replace the writer behind the same function (open question 1). In SwiftUI: VisionKit's `VNDocumentCameraViewController` where it is available (iOS on a device). It brings its own capture, corner and review screens, so these mockups are the web's and the fallback's. Elsewhere (macOS, the simulator) it uses the photo picker, Vision's rectangle detection, Core Image's perspective correction and these screens. Its PDF comes from PDFKit. All of these are system frameworks, not packages.
- **Fixtures**: skewed-page photos whose expected corners are asserted within 1 % of the image width, and the corrected page's size. The three `cam-*.svg` and `page-*.svg` here are the drawn stand-ins. There is also a `blocks check --shot` example of the field row, and the stress set: a long label, empty, and the page limit.
- **JS off**: the static page writes a plain `<input type=file accept=image/* capture multiple>`, as upload does, and the app gets the photos instead of a PDF. It isn't drawn here, because it is the same as upload's static row.
- `scan.css` (tokens only) goes into the stylesheet folder. It reuses upload's `.fu`, `.fui`, `.pgt` and the button. `page.css` and `scan.js` are only for these previews. To make the images, run `./render.sh` (Playwright's cached headless Chromium, light and dark, reduced motion).
