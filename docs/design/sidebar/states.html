sidebar · every part and state

1 · The same sidebar, rail and expanded

The rail is the collapsed form: icons only, group titles become a thin divider, badges sit on the icon’s corner, the current page keeps its 3 px ink tick. Expanded (hover, focus or pinned) it shows labels, group titles, open groups’ children and the pin button. The labels are still in the rail for screen readers; only their drawing is hidden.

2 · Current page inside a group

A group with children is a toggle, not a link: tapping it opens or closes its list (▸ / ▾, aria-expanded). When the current page is a child of a closed group, or the sidebar is a rail, the parent carries the current tick so you can always see where you are. The app may open a group up front; the renderer opens the group holding the current page.

3 · Badges

A count in the house tag (mute frame), or with the bad tone in --r for “needs attention”. Counts over 99 read 99+, with the true number in the accessible name. On the rail the tag sits on the icon’s top right corner. A parent shows its own badge; it does not add up its children.

4 · Keyboard

Tab from the page moves into the sidebar; focus inside a rail opens it over the page like hover does, so a focused link always shows its label. Each link and toggle has the house 2 px ink ring. Pin is a toggle button (aria-pressed, named “pin sidebar” / “unpin sidebar”). Escape closes an open-over sidebar and returns focus to the link it came from.

5 · Stress

Long labels, group titles and the account line end in “…” on one line, the full text in a title tooltip and the accessible name. Many rows scroll inside the sidebar; the brand row and the footer stay put. With no links the body says the block’s empty text (“No pages yet” by default). A link with no icon shows its label’s first letter.
